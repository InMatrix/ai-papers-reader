Title: Unlocking Any-Order Generation in Pretrained Autoregressive Image Models
PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39293
Summary: Autoregressive (AR) image generators are powerful in principle but inflexible in practice: training with a fixed raster-scan order hardcodes a single factorization of the image distribution into the model, leaving it unable to inpaint, outpaint, or edit at inference time. Existing solutions, including the modern discrete-diffusion family, train from scratch on a mixture of token orderings, paying roughly 3x the compute of standard raster-scan training; discrete diffusion further sacrifices compatibility with the AR inference ecosystem (KV caching, vLLM) by construction. We argue that this cost is unnecessary. A pretrained raster-scan AR model has already learned the joint distribution over image tokens; what it lacks is not knowledge but the ability to express that knowledge through non-sequential conditional pathways. We propose a recipe for adapting pretrained raster-scan models into any-order generators using two components: future-aware positional embeddings that resolve a target-position identifiability problem at negligible overhead, and an ordering curriculum that traces a stable optimization path from the pretrained solution to a good any-order basin. Applied to four base models across two architectures and two scales (LlamaGen-L/XL, RAR-L/XL), the recipe substantially outperforms MaskGIT on ImageNet inpainting (FID 6.21 vs. 10.44) and outpainting, is competitive with MAR-L, trades a 1.2-1.9 increase in class-conditional FID for this flexibility, and costs 13-33% additional compute beyond pretraining, while leaving the standard AR inference stack untouched. Inpainting, outpainting, and editing, our results suggest, need not be added to autoregressive image models. They can be unlocked from the ones that exist. Code is available at https://github.com/Ordered-AR/unlockedadaptation.

Title: Matched Capabilities, Different Behaviors: A Controlled Study of MCP and CLI Interfaces for Language Agents
PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34298
Summary: Language agents increasingly act through external tools and interactive environments, making the interface between the model and its executable capabilities part of the decision process. Together, these results establish interface representation as part of the closed-loop decision environment shaping agent behavior.

Title: Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs
PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39302
Summary: Whether models trained on different modalities converge to the same representation of the world is central to AI. Even that rotation needs no pair: on the latest pair-free benchmark, our text JEPA beats every zero-shot accuracy reported with language models up to 8B parameters, e.g., 64\% against at most 56\% top-5 on ImageNet-100. Positive views, and not scale alone, are thus the lever to build platonic representations.

Title: AB-Attention: Accurate Block Attention for Accelerating Video Diffusion Transformers
PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39663
Summary: Video diffusion transformers generate high-quality videos, but the quadratic cost of 3D full attention dominates their inference. Dynamic block-sparse attention reduces this cost by computing exact attention only on key/value blocks selected from current activations. However, its fidelity degrades under aggressive sparsity: blocks are scored from coarse summaries or formed by costly global clustering, pruned blocks vanish from the softmax, and whole tiles are kept or dropped even when only a few of their keys matter. To address these limitations, we propose Accurate Block Attention (AB-Attention), a training-free method that accurately approximates dense attention at high sparsity. Title: Multi-Token Prediction Done Right
PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38331
Summary: Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) is now a common ingredient in language-model pre-training, as it improves downstream performance and enables faster inference through speculative decoding. We identify a flaw in standard MTP training, in which a residual component of the MTP gradient interferes with both next-token and multi-token learning, increasingly so with scale. Specifically, the MTP cross-entropy gradient can be decomposed exactly into a KL self-distillation term that pulls each MTP head toward the model's own next-token prediction (NTP) distribution and improves speculative decoding, and a residual that has a low signal-to-noise ratio and buries the useful KL signal. We introduce the KL gradient-recovery trick: when two distributions share a linear output head, the gradient of their KL divergence can be recovered exactly by combining their cross-entropy gradients through straight-through gradient routing. Unlike minimizing the KL directly, this trick requires no vocabulary-sized logit matrices and is compatible with standard fused cross-entropy kernels. This enables Soft Multi-Token Prediction (Soft-MTP), a one-line code change to MTP that removes the noisy residual and backpropagates only the KL self-distillation signal by reusing the existing NTP and MTP cross-entropy computations. Soft-MTP supports both DeepSeek- and Meta-style MTP, adds no parameters, and incurs no measurable overhead. From 167M to 1.2B parameters, Soft-MTP improves over standard MTP with lower NTP perplexity, faster speculative decoding and higher zero-shot accuracy at every scale, lower MTP perplexity from 689M onward, and stronger math and code reasoning after fine-tuning at 1.2B. Soft-MTP also matches, if not exceeds, direct KL divergence minimization on most metrics. Soft-MTP is, in effect, a free lunch for MTP optimization. Our code can be found at https://github.com/JaydenTeoh/Soft-MTP.

Title: CausGround: Causal Interaction Grounding for Adaptive Embodied Planning
PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.29702
Summary: Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) show promising reasoning capabilities, but reliable physical interaction requires planning that accounts for when actions are executable and how they change the world. These preconditions and effects are environment-dependent and often not captured by MLLMs, making adaptation challenging. Yet existing approaches either require costly environment-specific training or rely on unstructured interaction experience that is difficult to reuse. Therefore, we introduce CausGround, a non-parametric causal interaction grounding framework that distills interaction experience into reusable action-centered causal knowledge. CausGround identifies action preconditions and effects and organizes them in a factorized world-state representation capturing object attributes and inter-object relations. At planning time, task-relevant causal dependencies are grounded in current observations to support backward reasoning over preconditions and forward reasoning over effects for action selection and multi-step planning. We evaluate CausGround on simulated high-level planning, interaction reasoning, and real-world robot manipulation. On adapted VirtualHome and RoboCasa benchmarks, CausGround improves vision-based high-level planning across MLLM backbones, increasing average task success from 24.9% to 53.3% on VirtualHome and closed-loop planning success from 43.0% to 57.0% on RoboCasa. On SWITCH, average interaction reasoning accuracy increases from 25.8% to 31.9%, indicating improved action selection, recovery, and verification. Moreover, real-world experiments show that CausGround reasons backward from MLLM-interpreted goals to derive executable high-level action sequences for sequential manipulation. These results demonstrate that reusable action preconditions and effects support long-horizon embodied planning across diverse environments without parameter updates.

Title: HuGe: Scaling Humanoid Generalist Vision-Language-Action Models with Egocentric Experience
PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34186
Summary: Extending Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) to humanoid robots requires coordinated whole-body control and diverse demonstrations that are costly to collect in real-world scenarios. Although egocentric human demonstrations provide a complementary source of task experience, differences in visual embodiment and missing robot proprioception complicate transfer to humanoid policies. To address these challenges, we introduce HuGe, a family of Humanoid Generalist VLAs pre-trained on over 2,000 hours of egocentric human and robot data. Specifically, HuGe employs three existing representative VLA architectures to predict latent motion tokens for a pretrained whole-body controller, together with hand-control signals. Based on this, we introduce a three-stage training pipeline that combines egocentric pre-training, humanoid adaptation, and downstream fine-tuning. To facilitate transfer across embodiments, we construct pseudo-proprioceptive states through simulation playback during pre-training and modify arm appearance in egocentric observations through re-rendering during downstream fine-tuning. With this pipeline, HuGe achieves the state-of-the-art on simulation benchmark HumanoidArena. Furthermore, real-world experiments on the Unitree G1 indicate that augmenting robot demonstrations with visually adapted egocentric demonstrations significantly increases success on a pick-and-drop task.

Title: OmniSpectra: A Unified Foundation Model for Universal Spectra Representation Learning
PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.15351
Summary: Standard sequence models encode position by token rank rather than physical coordinate, typically forcing practitioners to destructively resample continuous physical signals such as spectra observed across heterogeneous instruments. We identify this coordinate mismatch as a structural limitation in cross-instrument representation learning, and propose OmniSpectra, a wavelength-native foundation model for spectral representation learning: a continuous-coordinate masked autoencoder that discards the integer pixel indexing of sequence modeling. Mapping physical coordinates to a logarithmic basis turns the multiplicative scaling induced by cosmological redshift into a uniform additive shift. In continuous rotary position embeddings, this shared shift cancels from relative token distances rather than producing grid-dependent positional distortion. We further counter the spectral bias inherent to implicit neural decoders with a physics-grounded, resolution-gated Fourier decoder that provides anti-aliasing. Pretrained on eight surveys and benchmarked against seven baselines, our model reaches state-of-the-art performance with only 12.2M parameters while outperforming substantially larger models, indicating that encoding physical structure can substitute for parameter scale.
