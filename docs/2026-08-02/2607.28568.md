title: AI Model Takes a Step Toward Recursive Self-Improvement by Learning to Build
  Better ML Systems
---



Researchers at Frontis.AI and Tsinghua University have released an open system that brings AI one step closer to improving the very process of building AI. The system, called OpenMLE, trains a model to write, debug, refine, and recombine machine-learning programs through a loop of execution feedback. The result, Frontis-MA1-35B, reaches a 71.21% medal average on MLE-Bench Lite under a modest 12-hour, single-RTX 4090 budget—outperforming GPT-5.5 paired with Codex.

The core idea is to treat machine learning engineering itself as a testbed for recursive self-improvement. Rather than just generating one solution, the model performs four atomic operations: Draft, Improve, Debug, and Crossover. These are trained from verified execution outcomes, then composed into long-horizon evolutionary search. In effect, the model acts as both the engineer and the variation engine: it proposes solutions, tests them in a sandbox, learns from what worked, and recombines successful ideas.

A concrete example shows why this matters. On a leaf-classification task, Frontis-MA1 used Debug to fix multiclass label encoding and unstable cross-validation folds. Later, Crossover fused a ResNet18 image branch with 192 engineered tabular features into a hybrid model. A final Improve step upgraded the fused representation to ConvNeXt-Tiny embeddings, boosting validation Human Rank from 0.3534 to 0.7713 and earning a Bronze medal. On an audio bird-detection task, the model repaired a broken submission, built separate audio branches, and recombined them into a robust pipeline that won Silver.

The search process also learns from experience more efficiently. Instead of showing every model an ever-growing log, OpenMLE-Evo stores structured "experience cards" for each candidate and selects parents using three factors: score, progress, and novelty. This prevents a promising but slightly lower-scoring solution from being discarded. In the right-whale detection case, a parent ranked sixth by score but first by improvement carried valuable temporal audio features; the three-factor selection kept it alive, and a child derived from it reached 0.99386 held-out AUC.

The gains transfer beyond Kaggle-style competitions. On a 10-task NatureBench Lite subset spanning scientific domains, both the post-trained model and the adapted evolutionary harness improved results. Holding the framework fixed, swapping in Frontis-MA1 raised Match-SOTA from 50% to 70%; holding the model fixed, swapping in OpenMLE-Evo raised it from 20% to 50%.

OpenMLE is not full recursive self-improvement. The evolutionary system itself remains largely fixed, and the model only optimizes external ML artifacts, not its own weights or harness. But by releasing the task environments, training code, sandbox infrastructure, and model weights, the team has made a rare, reproducible step toward executable AI4AI—where AI systems genuinely learn to build better AI.
