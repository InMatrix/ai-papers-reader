title: Qwen-UI-Agent Shows a New Kind of AI Assistant That Can Actually Get Things
  Done Across Your Devices
---



Imagine your phone receives a flight-cancellation notice at 11 p.m. Before you even open the airline app, an AI agent has already checked alternative flights and high-speed trains, compared them against your 2 p.m. meeting, and prepared a decision-ready rebooking plan for your approval. That is the vision behind Qwen-UI-Agent, a new foundation model from Alibaba’s Qwen team designed to operate real phones, desktops, browsers, and search tools as easily as a human would.

The paper, released as a technical report on arXiv, describes an agent that moves beyond the usual demo-friendly benchmarks. Many GUI agents are trained in sanitized simulators and fail on real devices, where pop-ups, CAPTCHAs, stale logins, and unpredictable app states interrupt execution. Qwen-UI-Agent tackles this simulation-to-real gap by building a runtime with more than 100 physical phones and over 150 apps, using real-device trajectories for training and evaluation. On the new MobileWorld-Real benchmark, it scores 92.2%, beating frontier models like Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.6 Sol. On AndroidDaily, it reaches 97.5%.

A central idea is that GUI agents shouldn’t be limited to clicking and typing. Qwen-UI-Agent combines GUI actions with a command-line interface and API calls in one unified action space, and can batch multiple actions per model turn. In desktop tasks, over 40% of its actions are batched, substantially reducing the number of steps. For example, in one computer-use task the agent needed to find a Hong Kong photo and weather filters among many candidates. Instead of opening each image one by one, it wrote a small Python script to stitch all candidates into a single montage, inspected it visually at a glance, then used GIMP and PowerPoint to finish the job. In another task, it played a browser-based dinosaur game by writing a CLI controller that watches screenshots and presses the jump key—achieving a score of 156 without touching developer tools.

The model also learns from reinforcement learning over long trajectories exceeding 100 steps. This leads to behavioral shifts: after online RL, the agent verifies its own work more often, such as reopening a spreadsheet to confirm a chart actually rendered instead of trusting a script’s output. It also learns to use Bash as “hands” and GUI as “eyes,” efficiently executing file operations while visually inspecting receipts to distinguish income from expenses.

Perhaps most striking is proactive service. The harness layer watches mobile notifications, infers context from calendars, maps, and user preferences, and initiates tasks without being asked. In one demo, a flight cancellation triggers a cross-platform workflow: search flights via API, rebook through the mobile GUI after user approval, update a meeting schedule on the desktop, and send a revised spreadsheet to colleagues.

With state-of-the-art results on mobile, competitive scores on OSWorld and WebArena, and strong GUI grounding, Qwen-UI-Agent offers a clear picture of where assistant AI is headed: from reactive chatbots to agents that live on your devices and act before you ask.
